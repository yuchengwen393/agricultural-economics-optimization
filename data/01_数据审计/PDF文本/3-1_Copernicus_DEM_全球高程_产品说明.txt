Copernicus DEM - Global and European Digital Elevation Model
The Copernicus DEM is a Digital Surface Model (DSM) that represents the surface of the Earth including buildings, infrastructure and vegetation.The Copernicus DEM is provided in 3 different instances: EEA-10, GLO-30 and GLO-90.
Data were acquired by the TanDEM-X mission between 2011 and 2015. If any Copernicus DEM products show a sensing date earlier than 2011,this is because older elevation models were used to fill data gaps. To make sure you find all available data in the Copernicus Browser, it isrecommended to extend the search time range back to 2001. The datasets were made available for use in 2019 and will be maintained until2026.
All registered users (i.e. How to register to access Copernicus Contributing Missions data (https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/explore-data/data-
collections/copernicus-contributing-missions/ccm-how-to-register) ) can freely access the 30m and 90m resolution instances of the Copernicus DEM.Access to the 10m resolution instances over Europe is limited to a specific subset of users, as outlined in the ESA-User license for the use ofCCM (https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/sites/default/files/media/files/2025-06/copernicus_contributing_mission_data_access_v2_cop_dem_licenses.pdf) data and CCMData Access Restrictions (https://dataspace.copernicus.eu/sites/default/files/media/files/2025-06/ccm_data_access_restrictions_v2.pdf) .
The Copernicus DEM is a Digital Surface Model (DSM) which represents the top-reflective surface of the Earth including buildings, infrastructureand vegetation. This DEM is derived from an edited DSM named WorldDEMTM , i.e. flattening of water bodies and consistent flow of rivers hasbeen included. Editing of shore- and coastlines, special features such as airports and implausible terrain structures has also been applied.In contrast to Digital Surface Models such as Copernicus DEM, a Digital Terrain Model (DTM) represents the Earth's terrain with buildings,infrastructure and vegetation being removed in a DEM editing process.Additionally, hybrid DEMs are possible with e. g. only vegetation or only buildings / infrastructure removed.
Further information on DEM types is available in Peter Guth et.al.: Digital Elevation Models: Terminology and Definitions, 2021.
(https://www.mdpi.com/2072-4292/13/18/3581)The aforementioned differentiation should be considered when comparing DSMs, such as the current Copernicus DEM, with external referencedata (e.g. LiDAR-based DTM data).
EEA-10: European coverage
EEA-10 covers 39 European countries including all islands of those countries plus French Overseas Departments (excluding French OverseasTerritories) at a resolution of 10 metres with a surface coverage of ~ 6 M km 2 .
